## Section A

Answer all questions

### Question A.1

A manufacturer of chocolate bars claims that the mean mass of its Red77 bars is 100g. A researcher obtains a random sample of 40 Red77 bars and weighs them with an accurate weighing scale. The masses, in grams, of the bars in the sample can be found in the file red77.txt. Masses of Red77 bars may be assumed to be approximately Normally distributed.

(a) Using R, produce a histogram of the masses of the bars in the sample with 6 bins, using a vector to define the bin boundaries.

(b) Using R:

- (i) Test the alternative hypothesis that the mean mass of Red77 bars is not 100g against the null hypothesis that the mean mass is 100g. Explain clearly what test you are using, and any assumptions you are making. What is the p-value for your test? Discuss to what extent the test gives significant evidence to reject the null hypothesis.
- (ii) Generate a symmetric 99% confidence interval for the mean mass of Red77 chocolate bars.

### Question A.2

There are 4000 students at Humble College of Humanities (HCH). Each must take a science, engineering or mathematics course as part of their studies, from: Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Engineering, or Mathematics. The number of students with each main subject is shown in the table below:

| Subject | Number of students |
| --- | --- |
| Physics | 602 |
| Chemistry | 501 |
| Biology | 1412 |
| Engineering | 688 |
| Mathematics | 797 |

Using R, produce a bar chart to illustrate this data. Your chart should include a label for each bar.

### Question A.3

A researcher at Northeastern University wishes to know if there is evidence of a statistical association between people’s favourite colour (between red, blue and green) and their favourite drink (between beer, cider, orange juice and whisky), among adults living in London. She collects a random sample of 400 adults living in London, and they all respond to her invitation to participate in a survey. A table showing stated favourite drink against favourite colour, for the 400 people in the sample, is given below.

| | Red | Blue | Green | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beer | 65 | 30 | 25 | 120 |
| Cider | 40 | 20 | 20 | 80 |
| Orange juice | 35 | 25 | 40 | 100 |
| Whisky | 60 | 25 | 15 | 100 |
| Total | 200 | 100 | 100 | 400 |

Using R, carry out a chi-squared test of association on the data in the table. You should test the alternative hypothesis of an association between favourite drink and favourite colour, against the null hypothesis of no association. From the resulting p-value, comment on the extent to which the test result provides evidence to reject the null hypothesis.

## Section B

You should answer either question B.1 or question B.2.

### Question B.1

Write a program in R to generate, using the acceptance-rejection method, a random sample of (i) 20 observations, and (ii) 1,000,000 observations, from the continuous probability distribution with probability density f(x) given by:

```text
f(x) = 0.5           for 1 ≤ x ≤ 2
f(x) = 0.25(4 – x)   for 2 ≤ x ≤ 4
f(x) = 0             for all x < 1 and all x > 4
```

For the random sample of 20 observations, list them in ascending order. For the random sample of 1,000,000 observations, plot the density function using a kernel-based density estimate.

### Question B.2

This is designed to illustrate the Central Limit Theorem. For different sample sizes of observations from an Exponential distribution, it will show that the probability distribution for the sample mean gets progressively more like a Normal distribution as sample size increases. Working in R, you should:

(a) Write a function to generate sample means of random observations from an Exponential distribution with a rate constant (hazard rate) of λ = 0.2. The sample size n should be an argument of the function.

(b) Using your function in (a), plot the distribution of the sample means generated by (a) for sample sizes of 5, 10, 20, 50 and 100, by in each case generating a suitably large number of observations of the sample mean and then using a kernel-based density estimate for the plot.
